Construction

The mark is built from one number: r, the circle radius. Everything else is a fraction of it, so the logo scales without redrawing.

Geometry

Circle radius r. Centres sit 0.52 r either side of the axis. The heart is 0.76 r tall, centred in the overlap, never touching an edge.

Clear space

Keep half a radius clear on every side of the mark or the full lockup. Nothing crosses it — not type, not a photo edge, not another logo.

Minimum size

Below 24 px the heart closes up. Below 120 px wide the wordmark's thin joins fill in — switch to the mark alone instead of shrinking further.

Never do this

The mark carries the whole idea in its geometry. Break the geometry and the idea goes with it.

Three of the brand colours can't hold text on a light background

Mint, teal and coral are surface and accent colours. When the same colour has to be a word — a link, a label, an error — use its deep shade. These are the only additions to the palette, and each clears 4.5:1 on Soft Cream.

Engraving

Money has its own graphic tradition: the guilloché line work engraved into banknotes and share certificates. Every curve below is generated from 0.52 — the same ratio that positions the two circles in the mark. The identity and the security pattern come from one number.

Rosette Watermark behind a balance, statement headers, card backs. Never above 50% opacity.
Border braid Two frequencies in a 13:25 relationship. Section rules, receipt edges, export headers.
Security field The mark itself, hairlined and half-dropped. Empty states, sign-in, locked screens.

Currency and cents sit up

The symbol drops to 58% and rises 0.46em; cents drop to 56% and rise 0.58em. The dollars stay the loudest thing in the number, which is what people actually read.

Figures are always tabular

Every numeral occupies the same width, so a column of amounts holds its alignment while values change. Never use proportional figures for money.

Minus, not brackets

A true minus sign (−) marks money out. Accounting brackets read as a spreadsheet, not a life. Colour never carries the sign on its own.

Voice

Two people share this screen and they may not agree about money. Pairance reports; it never takes a side and never scolds.

Say what happened, plainly

SayGroceries went $180 over this month.
NotUh oh! Someone's been overspending 😬

Never assign blame

SayTwo purchases aren't categorised yet.
NotSam forgot to categorise these again.

Empty screens invite action

SayNo goals yet. Add the first one you both want.
NotNothing to show here.

Errors explain the fix

SayYour bank ended the connection. Sign in again to resume syncing.
NotSorry! Something went wrong.
